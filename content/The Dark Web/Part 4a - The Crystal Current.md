> ### THE WAR OF REVENGE: A CONFLICT IN ITS FINAL CHAPTER
>
> _By Talindra Gaelithar, Senior Correspondent, Crystal Current Wartime Bureau_
>
> For nearly a decade, the Known Spheres have endured the shadow of a war that many believed would never come. The Second Unhuman War, called the "War of Revenge" by the scro forces who launched it, has stretched across crystal spheres and through the Phlogiston itself, consumed fleets, and reshaped the political landscape of the Known Spheres in ways that will be studied for centuries. But as the conflict enters what military analysts and IEF commanders alike describe as its twilight phase, one truth has become difficult to deny: the war, for all its devastation, is ending. And it is ending on elven terms.
>
> To understand the present, we must look to the past. The roots of this conflict reach back over four hundred years to the conclusion of the First Unhuman War, sometimes called the Great Hunt, in which the Elven Imperial Fleet dismantled the goblinoid fleets that had terrorized the Phlogiston for generations. It was a decisive, total campaign. Orc, goblin, hobgoblin, kobold, ogre, and bugbear fleets were shattered across every major sphere. Many were driven from the spacelanes entirely, scattered to isolated worlds where they were expected to remain grounded forever. The Fleet emerged as the uncontested dominant force in all the Known Spheres, a position it held without serious challenge for four centuries.
>
> What the Fleet did not account for was the tenacity of hatred.
>
> Among the survivors of that first war was a tribe of orcs who retreated to a distant, uncharted crystal sphere and settled on a world they would come to name after their leader: Dukagsh. This figure, part warlord and part philosopher, recognized that the goblinoid races had lost not because they lacked strength or numbers, but because they lacked discipline, unity, and vision. Under his guidance, these orcs were transformed. They trained in tactics. They studied strategy. They built a culture around martial precision and collective purpose. Over the centuries, they evolved, both culturally and physically, into something new. They called themselves the scro, the name of their old race reversed, a symbol of reinvention.
>
> For hundreds of years, the scro expanded quietly, careful never to draw the attention of the Fleet. They recruited sympathizers and disaffected groups to their cause, including the bionoids, living weapons originally engineered by elven arcanists and then discarded once they had outlived their usefulness. Though no spacefaring nation formally aligned with the scro, the resentment that many races harbored toward elven authority was enough to buy them neutrality. And neutrality, in wartime, is its own kind of alliance. Others, such as the insectare, installed operatives on both sides, content to watch two great powers exhaust one another.
>
> The war announced itself not with a single declaration but with a pattern. Isolated IEF outposts began to disappear. Patrol ships failed to report in. Supply convoys vanished between spheres, swallowed by the Phlogiston as though it had simply closed behind them. Each incident was made to look like an accident, or the work of common pirates. By the time Fleet Command recognized the pattern for what it was, the scro had already established bases of operation across multiple crystal spheres and were preparing for open conflict.
>
> The war spread in earnest to Realmspace around the year 1360 DR, and to Greyspace roughly two years later, though secret attacks and skirmishes had been recorded in outlying spheres for as many as five years prior. The scro Tarantula Fleet, under the command of Admiral Halker and General Vorr, launched a devastating invasion of the elven world of Spiral, employing vast numbers of goblin and orc auxiliaries alongside their own disciplined scro and ogre marines. The fall of Spiral was a blow to elven morale that echoed through every sphere, though the native population, elves, dwarves, and humans alike, mounted a fierce resistance from the planet's mountains and deep forests that has never been fully suppressed.
>
> The lowest point of the war for the Fleet came in Greyspace with what military historians now call the Borka Maneuver. A force of seventy-five scro Mantis-class warships, vessels designed specifically to counter the speed and agility of the elven Man-o-War, ambushed the Greyspace Fleet of sixty-nine ships, including twenty-four Armadas. The battle was catastrophic. The Fleet lost over half its vessels before the engagement ended. It was the single greatest loss the IEF had suffered since the founding of Lionheart.
>
> But it was also the scro's most pyrrhic triumph. The Borka Maneuver cost them sixty of those seventy-five ships. It was a hammer blow delivered by a force that could not afford to swing twice. And this is the essential arithmetic of the War of Revenge that the scro's leadership has never been able to solve: the Fleet can be hurt, but it cannot be broken, because behind the Fleet stands the combined industrial, arcane, and spiritual infrastructure of elven civilization across dozens of crystal spheres. The scro, for all their discipline, fight with what they have. The elves fight with what they can build.
>
> In the years since the Borka Maneuver, the trajectory of the war has shifted decisively. Fleet intelligence reports indicate that scro forces are increasingly reliant on mercenary auxiliaries and conscripted goblinoid levies to fill the gaps in their ranks. Their purpose-built warships, the Mantis, the Blade, the Battlewagon, were formidable innovations, but innovation means nothing without the shipyards to replace losses. Scro vessels captured in recent engagements have shown signs of deferred maintenance, improvised repairs, and cannibalized components, all hallmarks of a navy running out of time.
>
> Meanwhile, the Fleet has adapted. The initial shock of facing purpose-built counter-elven warships has given way to revised tactical doctrine, improved coordination between sphere fleets, and the deployment of new arcane countermeasures developed by the Honored Mages' Guild of Bral. The reinforcement of the Realmspace Fleet by the spelljammer forces of Evermeet proved a turning point in that sphere, resulting in more than a dozen engagements that forced a sustained scro retreat over approximately two years.
>
> This is not to suggest the road has been without setbacks. The Battle of Sector Nine stands as a sobering reminder that a wounded adversary is not a defeated one. IEF forces have regrouped since the initial battle, and the responsible criminal General Shaw Thunderwind has been killed.
>
> The cost of this war, however, cannot be measured only in ships lost and battles won. There is a weariness now that did not exist in the war's early years. It can be felt in the Phlogiston itself, in the longer silences between transmissions, in the way that celebrations of tactical victories have grown muted even among those who understand their importance. The scro, too, show signs of fatigue. Intercepted communications suggest growing tensions between scro high command and their goblinoid auxiliaries, disputes over resource allocation, and a recognition among some senior officers that the war's original objectives may no longer be achievable. Neither side speaks openly of peace. But both sides, for the first time, speak in the language of exhaustion.
>
> The question that hangs over the Known Spheres is no longer whether the war will end, but what form its ending will take, and what will be required to bring it about. The Fleet's Strategic Council has outlined scenarios ranging from a negotiated armistice to a sustained campaign of attrition that would grind scro forces down sphere by sphere over the coming years. The people of the Known Spheres, those who have borne the economic burden, the displacement, and the grief, are watching. They have endured much. They have been patient. But patience, like any resource, is finite, and the voices asking what it will take to bring this to a close grow louder with each passing quarter.
>
> The war is not over. But it is ending. And when the histories of this era are composed, they will record that the IEF, tested as it has never been tested before, met the challenge and held. The spacelanes will remain open. The Phlogiston will carry travelers in peace once again. And the legacy of those who served, on every vessel and in every sphere, will be honored for as long as the stars burn in the Void.

> ### SINDIATH LINE ANNOUNCES PORTFOLIO RESTRUCTURING ACROSS FOUR SUBSIDIARIES
>
> _By Jorell Ammath, Crystal Current Business Desk_
>
> The Sindiath Line's corporate governance board has confirmed a strategic restructuring of investment allocations across four of its subsidiary holdings, effective at the start of the next commercial quarter.
>
> The affected subsidiaries are Ainscorp, Greela Translight Logistics, Verdance Arcanomateriel, and Dawnthread Communications. All four have historically maintained significant service contracts with the IEF, providing fleet provisioning, arcane component procurement, long-range communications infrastructure, and supply chain management
>
> In a statement released through the Line's Office of Commercial Affairs, the restructuring was described as "a forward-looking realignment of capital priorities." Ainscorp, the largest of the four affected subsidiaries and a cornerstone of the Line's fleet logistics network, will reportedly scale back its direct provisioning contracts of the IEF by an undisclosed yet significant margin. Ainscorp's Director of External Relations, Vaelith Sunspar, characterized the shift as "a natural evolution of our commercial strategy."
>
> The Fleet's Office of Procurement declined to comment, stating only that "the Fleet maintains robust and diversified supply relationships across the Known Spheres and is confident in its logistical readiness."
>
> Industry observers have noted the restructuring follows a broader trend among Sindiath Line affiliates, as several smaller subsidiaries have quietly reduced their exposure to military-adjacent contracts in recent quarters. The Crystal Current will continue to monitor developments.
