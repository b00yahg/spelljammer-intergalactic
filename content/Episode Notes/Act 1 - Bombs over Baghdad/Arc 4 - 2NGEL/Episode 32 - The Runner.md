We open the session with a captain's log, written by an Elvish Imperial Navy captain who landed on [[Athas]] and discovered defiling magic.

# Captain's Log

> Captain's Log.
> Thalindra Starwarden.
> Commander, IEN Verdant Thorn.
> Seventh Astral Armada, Imperial Elven Navy.
>
> Survey Day One.
>
> We have breached the sphere and entered orbit above a world the cartographers have designated Athas. A single sun, closer and more punishing than any I have encountered in thirty years of service.
>
> Our mission here is one of contact and assessment. The Imperial Elven Navy has long known that elven populations exist on worlds beyond our reach, scattered across spheres we have yet to chart, and it is the Navy's standing mandate to seek them out, to learn of their condition, and where possible, to extend the hand of the Empire. To bring them prosperity, protection, and unity under a common banner. Athas has been flagged as a world of particular interest due to reports of a significant elven presence, and we have been tasked with making first contact, evaluating their society, and determining whether diplomatic overtures would be welcomed.
>
> The helmsman reports an unusual resistance in the spelljamming current as we approach the planet, a thickness he cannot quite articulate, but I have logged the anomaly and see no reason for concern. We descend at dawn. I expect a standard survey, perhaps a tenday, and then home.
>
> Captain's Log.
> Survey Day Four.
> I find myself revising nearly every assumption we brought with us into this sphere.
>
> We have made landfall in a region of cracked bedrock and low dunes, south of what appears to be a walled city of considerable size, and the conditions here are beyond anything the reports prepared us for. The heat is extraordinary, oppressive in a way that feels almost deliberate, and two of my crew are already showing signs of dehydration despite strict ration compliance.
>
> More troubling, however, is what we have observed of the local elven population. They are tall, lean to the point of severity, and they move across open desert at a dead run, mile after mile, under a sun that would incapacitate my crew within hours. They carry weapons of black glass and sharpened bone. I have seen no metal anywhere on this world. There are no temples, no shrines, no holy symbols of any recognizable kind, and my divination officer attempted a standard communion this morning and reported not silence but absence, as though the divine channel simply does not exist here.
>
> I came to this world believing we would find elves in need of the Empire's guidance. What I am finding instead is a people who appear to have built an entire civilization without any of the things we consider essential to one.
>
> Captain's Log.
> Survey Day Nine.
> Our position has been compromised, and with it, I believe, the mission itself.
>
> A scouting party of local elves discovered our camp at dawn. Their leader, a woman with pale eyes and a scar running from temple to jaw, stood at the perimeter and simply waited, watching us with a patience that felt more like assessment than curiosity.
>
> When she spoke, her Common was functional but stripped of every courtesy, every diplomatic nicety I have been trained to employ. I told her who we were. Elves, like them, from beyond the sky. I explained that the Imperial Navy had sent us to learn of her people and to offer the friendship and prosperity of a great elven kingdom that spans the stars.
>
> She looked at me for a long time, and then she used a word in her own tongue that my linguist translates roughly as "the soft ones." She accepted our water rations and left without further discussion.
>
> That evening, Ensign Calavar wandered from the perimeter and was found at dawn, stripped of every useful possession, killed with clean obsidian cuts. I do not know if it was the same elves or another group entirely. I am told there are many tribes here, and that raiding is not a transgression but a way of life. The Empire's hand of friendship, it seems, is not something this world recognizes.
>
> Captain's Log.
> Survey Day Fourteen.
> The helmsman is dead of heatstroke, and I have performed the rites over his body, though I confess I do not know whether they carry any weight on a world where no god appears to be listening. Our water reserves, calculated for a tenday, are nearly gone. I rationed too conservatively, too late.
>
> This morning, in what I now recognize as desperation, I attempted to cast a sending to the Seventh Armada to request extraction. The spell fired, and I felt the energy leave me as it always does, but something else happened with it. The ground beneath my feet turned to powder. Grey ash in a perfect circle around my boots, and the scrub grass that had been clinging to life in the cracks of the rock simply ceased to exist.
>
> My magic did not draw from the Weave, because there is no Weave here. It drew from the land itself, and the land died to pay for it.
>
> I have served the Navy for three decades. I have charted spheres where the stars themselves sing. I have never been afraid of magic until today. I will not cast again.
>
> Captain's Log.
> Survey Day Nineteen.
> I am alone. Lieutenant Vaelis took our remaining water in the night and I do not blame her for it.
>
> I am sitting against the hull of the Verdant Thorn, which is hot enough to blister skin through cloth, and I lack the strength to climb to the helm. I came to this world on behalf of the Imperial Elven Navy to find our kin, to evaluate their readiness for unity, to offer them the light of an empire that has brought prosperity to elves across a dozen spheres.
>
> I understand now how foolish that was. The elves of Athas do not need our prosperity. They do not want our unity.
>
> They have become something the Empire has no framework to comprehend, something forged in conditions so hostile that the very concept of aid is an insult to what they have survived. They are not our kin. They are what our kin become when every comfort is taken, every softness punished, and the only things left are the things that keep you alive for one more day. If this log reaches the Seventh Armada, I ask that Command hear me clearly: do not send another survey. Do not send diplomats. Do not send soldiers. Do not come here.
>
> \[The recording shifts. A new voice. Lower. Rougher. Common spoken like a blade held wrong on purpose. Behind it, wind. The faint crackle of something burning.]
>
> You found this. Good. You were meant to.
>
> I am Vakarra. Fire Dagger Clan. Silt Stalkers. Daughter of no one worth naming. I speak for the dead and for the living and for the sand between.
>
> Your soft one came here. Pretty ship. Glass instruments. Star charts. She wanted to study us. Write us down. Carry us back to whatever green and gentle world she crawled out of. She is dead now. The sun took her. It takes everything that does not belong here.
>
> We did not kill her. We did not need to. Athas did the work. She came to learn about us. So learn. We do not herd. We do not farm. We do not trade unless the trade is blood for water. We take what we need from the ones too slow to stop us and we do not mourn what we leave behind. We were not given this world. We were left with it. And we did not break. We did not beg. We did not pray to gods that will never answer because there are no gods to answer. We sharpened what was left. And we kept running.
>
> You call yourselves elves. You call us elves. You think that word means something. It does not. Not here. Here there is no name that matters except the one you carve into the ones who come for you. So hear me, star people.
>
> If you heard your soft one was here to learn about us, then know this. I come from the world of fire and ash. Bones, stone, and obsidian. That is what matters here.
>
> Do not bring your weakness. Do not bring your pity. Do not bring your mercy. Mercy is a luxury for people who have water to spare and shade to rest in. We have neither. If you come, come with strength.
>
> Come ready to be stripped down to what you actually are underneath all that silver and starlight.
>
> Or do not come at all.

# Session Notes

We cut to the present day. Our escorts bring us up to a ridge of broken shale. They do their special walk to ensure we don't attract more drakes.

We arrive at the elves' camp. Hunters and children walk between the tents. Walls enclose the encampment. A large firepit sits in the center of the camp. Guards watch our arrival from the top of the walls.

A guard calls out, asking Hekam what's going on here, and if we're from the city. Hekam replies that we claim to come from the sky like the blue dragon. That we wish to speak to Ivu.

Hekam says he can translate for us—but [[Otto]] replies that there's no need. He greets the gatekeeper and says that the gatekeeper is manning the finest gate we've seen on Athas. Kotuk (the gatekeeper) asks Hekam if Otto's going to explain why we're here or "if he's just trying to fuck me."

![[Images/Pasted image 20260520231409.png]]

[[Vayb]] steps in, smooths the situation a bit, and he and Hekam explain in a little more detail. Telar vouches for the prowess they witnessed from us. Kotuk allows us into the camp.

As we walk, Vayb telepathically contacts Telar and asks—"I didn't realize you were _directly_ dealing with [[2NGEL]]". She elaborates: "We are three clans united under one clan: the Fire Swords, warriors. The Fire Daggers, who work in espionage. The Fire Bows, the scouts. We are, together, a united force. The Fire Daggers work directly with 2NGEL, but it is under the approval of the Clan. 2NGEL works with a specific member of the clans, the Runner. It is the Runner who allows him to travel through the desert to his destination. What he does there, I do not know. I have been too terrified to do so. It is..." She thinks, then says an Elvish word that refers to a bad omen, or something best avoided.

"And no one in your clan has ever seen this place, have you?" He shoots Telar a knowing glance. She looks to Hekam and confesses that he _is_ nosy. "We all obey our chief, but we have different opinions here. Some think this is a bad idea, others do not care. It does serve to benefit us, and the relationship is purely transactional. For the chief, that is enough."

She also explains that there is contention behind the chief's ascension: that the current chief won leadership from the last one in a duel. That fact aggravates some, after the current arrangement began. At Vayb's prompting, she explains that this was a year ago.

"The Runner brought the dragon to us," she explains. "We were not welcoming at first, but the Runner convinced our chief to at least hear him out. And it's hard to argue with what he proposed." She points to something _very_ unusual in Athas: a _fountain_.

As for her disagreement with the chief's decision, Vayb surmises that's why she's so willing to explain their clan's story. She nods, looks over us, and says she hopes we can do something. [[Claire]], seeing them having a telepathic conversation, throws a peace sign.

We huddle and share this information and agree that the plan is to talk to the Chief, find the Runner, maybe Detect some Thoughts.

As we head to see the chief after this huddle, Telar also asks why two of our people look exactly the same, but one is better dressed. Vayb starts to ask if she's never heard of twins, but then catches himself "wait, which one do think is _better dressed_?" Telar cocks an eyebrow. "The one in armour to protect her from the elements, of course."

![[Images/Pasted image 20260520231446.png]]

Suddenly, an arrow flies at [[D'Arby]], which he dodges when his reflexes kick in. It's a blunted arrow, with a cushioned end. A voice speaks out. "Good reflexes! Great sign! Wonderful start!" A young elf strolls over with a jaunt in his step. He has copper skin, high cheekbones, and a perpetual grin. His hair is braided with obsidian chips in the braid, which click as he walks. He puts the arrow back in his quiver. He's speaking in Common.

He explains that he wanted to see what is up with these outsiders. That he had to test us first. Otto throws his baton at this guy, who catches it. The elf laughs and asks if Otto wants to challenge him. When Otto starts to try to turn him down, Miri (the elf) turns and makes a show to the elves of the camp, saying that one of the foreigners is trying to challenge him.

Otto turns more serious, telling Miri to give him one good reason to humour him or if he's just "empty air". Miri says that we'll need steady hands if we're headed to the crashed ship. Otto and Miri end up coming to an agreement and duel.

Miri's movement as he starts the fight is rushed—Otto's goading clearly got to him. But he moves oddly perfectly, hand coming to a standstill as he channels his frustration into the strike.

As the scuffle starts, we notice that elvish children have gathered and are gawking at Claire's hair, and looking at D'Arby. They whisper amongst themselves. Hekam translates in a whisper. He tells us that the kids are wondering that, if the one in white is capable of that, what can the one with no eyes and the one with fire hair do?

Claire hands D'Arby her phone, then approaches the kids and makes conversation. She gives them a necklace she looted at a previous point in the adventure, explaining that it's metal and gemstones (which she explains are "like fancy obsidian" and points skyward). D'Arby mutters that she can't just give gifts to random children like that. She asks them about the Runner and gets the directions to the Runner's tent, where she is apparently resting during the day because she runs at night.

![[Images/Pasted image 20260520231617.png]]

Meanwhile, Otto and Miri fight. Otto hands Miri his ass, and for the first time, Miri looks at Otto like he really believes Otto is an alien, for the first time. Otto offers Miri the same kind of advice he typically gives to [[Darrett]].

As this conversation winds down, we find a large, large man watching us from the tents nearby. He's built like a warrior, with scars on his fists.

He sees us notice him. Then approaches.

"You are the sky people?" He asks.

"I am from Balic, but I have visited the sky," Vayb replies. Then he points to Otto and D'Arby. "They came back with me."

The large elf gestures to [[Squall]]. "What of them?"

"That's complicated."

The elf grumbles in disapproval. Then, he switches to Common to address everyone. "The chief is resting. Can't see you."

"Then wake him up," D'Arby says, cranky in his sweat and ruined pompadour.

Vayb telepathically relays the message to Claire.

"That would be rude," replies the elf.

"Surely the chief can be roused to deal with a dire matter like this," says Otto.

"We don't want your sky treasures," replies the elf.

Vayb steps forward, explaining that we come to help, to do something more valuable than either bone or obsidian or water. "And you would be right to doubt those claims, so let us do something to prove them."

The elf considers.

Otto offers his own explanation: "2NGEL has done as much harm to us as he has to you and your community. We're in agreement. So we're here to take him down."

The elf sighs, then steps aside. "Not me you have to convince."

"I think I have to convince all of you," Vayb replies.

Meanwhile, Claire is skulking around and tries peering into the Runner's tent. Inside, she sees an elf whose skin is ashy and cracked. Her hair is shorn close on the sides but longer at the top. Her eyes are closed and her hands are pressed together. There's a dark purple glow around her: magic. The land withers around her.

She's casting some sort of ritual, a defilement. Claire snaps a few pictures of the ritual with her phone. Then she replies to Vayb and says "On my way, on my way, we have a defiler!"

Vayb looks at her with concern as she approaches—but Claire mainly looks pleased that she got this scoop herself. She shows him the picture. "Is that the Runner," he asks telepathically. She nods.

![[Images/Pasted image 20260520231533.png]]

Inside the tent, there is a red tarp on the floor, and finally we're in some nice shade. A pair of elves await us. The thinner one says they will translate everything that Ivo says. "I am Gadric, his advisor."

"We are the Ocean Court Express, and we are here to stop the one called 2NGEL, the blue dragon who is doing unnatural things in this world," says Otto.

The chief says that 2NGEL has been good to them, that he's given them means of survival for them and generations to come.

Squall points out that 2NGEL is trying to kill a lot of people.

"The dragon has made it clear that Athas will be left unchanged."

"And you're fine harbouring a fugitive?" Squall says.

"I don't think they care about their laws," Claire says.

"I don't care about your laws," says the chief.

Otto asks why they're okay making the trade of so many other lives for his community's.

There are three things in this world that matter, the chief says, bones, obsidian, and water.

"That's wrong," Vayb says. Squall asks about the lives of their fellows. "These guys don't understand freedom," Vayb surmises.

Claire says that we're trying to argue morality when this is business.

As we speak, Vayb spots tattoos on the translator's arms. Brands of House Tsalaxa. Former slave brands.

The translator asks if we want him to translate Vayb's words. Squall hesitates, but Vayb says "Yes, I meant what I said."

"A Wavir should choose his words carefully," says the translator.

"You know my name. Then you would know the Wavirs don't keep slaves," says Vayb.

Gadric explains that he's meant to advise the chief on matters of business. And that a unified front is a good thing, so he offers us an olive branch to get on the same page with each other.

Otto believes that anyone can be convinced to see the value of other life. Claire disagrees, and argues that we focus on his nature as a businessman—and on the elves' own morality. That they're harboring a defiler. Claire agrees with Otto, but not that that can be done quickly. Vayb agrees, that we probably don't have time. He sorta agrees with everyone, saying we try our best to appeal to him, but change gears as soon as we see the need.

Darrett realizes that D'Arby can't hear us and quietly explains that we've been talking the whole adventure and that he wants D'Arby to take off the ring of mind shielding so that he can be included. D'Arby replies that he can "hear the wind." And then just that he'll leap into action whenever the captains suggest.

Darrett sums up the thought, explaining that he thinks this chief does care about lives, just only those of the people around him. "Yeah," says Claire. "He's an elf." The group is shocked that Claire would say that. Either way, the group ends up on the same page and allows Vayb to press the translator angle first.

Meanwhile, D'Arby signs to Gadric. Gadric communicates in return, surprised that D'Arby knows it. Gadric explains that a friend lost their ears and this is how they talked. "That's no good," says D'Arby. D'Arby then asks Gadric to ask a direct question to the chief, from him personally. "Is this guy happy?"

Gadric asks it. The chief replies that victory is happiness. That the tribe has gotten stronger since he has ruled and that strength is safety and safety is survival. That any other kind of happiness in unfamiliar to him.

His eyes had been shifting between us as he speaks. But, as D'Arby takes off his sunglasses, the chief locks eye with D'Arby. D'Arby gives him a big look of genuine pity.

Then, Vayb speaks up. "Please tell your chief that what we offer is freedom. Freedom from defiling. 2NGEL works with the kings. What he does will suck the life dry from the land. What he does will betray you all. I do not ask for much. We just ask for a way through."

The chief tells us that strength is domination, and if our strength does NOT come from that, where could it possibly come from in order to be something to rely on.

Otto replies. Domination is not where strength comes from. It comes from purpose. That one's purpose might be to survive, or to dominate, or to protect those we care about. But true strength comes from a true purpose, untouched by selfishness, by the shortcuts that people like 2NGEL take.

The chief says that he has no love for the elf-slayer or any of his allies. "And if any of what is offered here is true, it has value." Then, he asks for the opinion of Gadric, his advisor.

"I think they're right," says Gadric. "You brought me into this tribe to tell you when I recognize deals. This man comes from the stars, from a world of infinite possibilities, and what does he have to offer you? Obsidian? Water? I believe he's short-changing you." Then he begins to lay into the chieftain, telling him that "you have been molded to his whims, grown to be putty in his hands. Naive, well-intentioned? Possibly. But these people offer freedom and all we have to do is offer these people next to nothing in return?"

The chieftain tells Gadric to order the Runner to lead us to the crashed ship.

Then, Claire presents the evidence she snapped of the Runner's defiling magic to Gadric and the chief, and asks if we can trust her. They're shocked by the revelation, but assure us that the Runner will follow the chief's orders.
