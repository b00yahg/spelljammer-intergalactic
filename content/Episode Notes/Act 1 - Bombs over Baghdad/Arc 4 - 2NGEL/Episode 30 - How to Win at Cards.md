The sky breaks apart.

The beam dies as the Ocean Court Express is teleported. Then...

Silence.

The ship finds an abyss of blood red around it. [[Athas]] is a dead world beneath us, reds and rusty browns. The Merkabah hangs in orbit above the red planet. Through a row of windows, light filters out. Shades of amber and sickly green.

The ship waits for its master on the planet below.

![[Images/Pasted image 20260411204709.png]]

[[Darrett]] asks how we plan to enter the ship. It seems to have its mouth open—[[Otto]] suggests we take up its invite, like a little ocean creature. This causes a tangent as [[Claire]] asks, surprised, if he doesn't know what krill are. [[Squall]] doesn't, either, and Darrett is proud to get to teach our group what something is for once. (Authour's note: notably, the Merkabah is a _sperm whale_, which doesn't even eat krill :p)

As for the specific approach, Claire suggests we avoid bringing our ship in range of the tractor beam and just board it personally from space. "We already broke its window once before, right?"

Otto suggests cracking open a window with our ship's gun. Darrett points out that that would alert anyone on the ship—and Otto compliments Darrett on his strategic thinking.

We keep planning until [[Vayb]] interrupts—admitting that he hasn't been paying attention to our debate. He says it's probably a trap, and all we can really do is spring the trap and work with whatever happens.

Vayb slowly pilots us closer—and observes. By the way the ship moves, he can tell that it has a _pilot_, not an automated system. We finalize the plan and move.

Vayb and Claire, with PIXIE's help, set up _unseen servants_ to man the guns. Then, we take flight.

We break through the window into 2NGEL's bar, where we fought before with its piano and liquor collection. We gather, record a nice collection and toast for Claire's vlogs—which she ends by looking at the camera and saying "we're coming for you, [[2NGEL]]." Vayb makes sure to use his _mage hand_ to get a nice wide shot of the group as we take a shot.

Then, Claire, Vayb, and Squall all cast _invisibility_ at different levels to begin our exploration.

We head into the halls beyond the bar and parlor, where an undead creature limps around wearing a guard's uniform. Before going too much further, we have Claire jack in to 2NGEL's ship and we begin... the return of [[Threadweaving - Hacking Mechanics for D&D 5th Edition|Threadweaving]]!

At this end of the ship, there's a hatch to the command center of the ship as well as what seems to be a door to the captain's quarters. We try to break open the hatch.

Vayb approaches the captain's door, which is made of dark, red wood and containing white speckles. The door is unlocked. Vayb enters, immediately knocks over his desk's chair, and begins riffling through the room. He finds a closet with 2NGEL's dusters, a white thread on the shelf, a safe with a keypad, and a chest by the bed. Also, there's something under the bed.

Vayb crawls down to look and finds that there's a plush pet bed underneath the bed proper, as well as a whip with crimson spikes at the end. Squall looks it over and determines it's a _flametongue_ whip.

We cut back to Otto struggling to open the hatch. He begins hacking at it with his rod.

As he does, D'Arby's been keeping watch. He notices something: the ceiling. There's a device hanging from it, and it's made of crystal in the shape of an eye. It's slowly turning, and is turning in our direction. Now that Otto's not invisible, we need to keep an eye on the camera.

Otto stays away, and keeps smacking the hatch. As he breaks it open, [[D'Arby]] feels _something_ at the back of his mind.

He flashes back to something.

It is... dark. He's in an old house. Things are blurry, but he remembers the darkness. The sounds of howling. The doors. _Metal_ doors, of this strange house, being hit. Over and over, almost to bursting. And he's there, at least a version of him, standing in front of others—putting himself between them and the doors.

Otto knocks the hatch open. He climbs. There are Xs marked on his maps. Red Xs in familiar spaces. Yellow Xs marked in a few places. Blue Xs _all over_ the galaxy, connected by blue lines. And two green Xs. One points to Station 9. The other points to something deep, far into the Weird.

Otto, one of the only ones of us who knows about the Weird, considers what he knows: even _mind flayers_ don't go that far into the Weird. It's possible for them to go that far in theory, sure... but even though they can go that far—a mind flayer still has to feed. That deep in the Weird, they still need to eat.

Station Nine, also, he'd know is a station in the middle of the Golden Triangle. A hub for entertainment, akin to space Vegas.

We enter the next room, the cockpit.

When we arrive, a voice speaks—asking us "creatures" what we're doing in the belly of the beast. Otto comments that this is more like the brain—the creature says that IT is more the brain here.

The chair swivels, and it's occupied.

By a sapient, speaking fox.

Vayb, still invisible, creeps in stealthily. The group follows. Claire needs a good angle to record this for the vlog.

Otto expresses surprise that the fox isn't familiar with his boss' encounters. He ought to know the captain of the Ocean Court Express.

"Ocean Court Express?" the fox asks. "You're the neanderthals who blew a hole in my ship!"

As Otto exchanges verbal spars with the fox, Squall levels their lightning launcher at him.

Otto asks the fox to give his name in turn—but he replies that his true name is incomprehensible to mortals like us. However, we can use his title. He explains that he is the Right Hand. Proverbially speaking, at least, as 2NGEL uses both his hands to dispense righteous fury—but the fox guides the hands and pilots the ship. He is beyond the number system used by 2NGEL, in a way, but he has claimed such a title. We can call him F0X.

Then, we get an encrypted message from [[L1CH]]. "He is not affiliated with us. This does not count. Not part of our group."

Talking of 2NGEL, F0X tells us about how he saw Theo grow from a boy to a bounty hunter to a man strong enough to claim what he wants. "Do you really think you have strength to rival that?"

"Rival?" Otto says. "We are five. We have eclipsed him long ago. Every time we've encountered Theo, we've left him running—all that is left is to finalize the transfer of power."

Otto tells F0X that 2NGEL's star is dimming, that the end is near and that F0X has the chance to switch sides now.

The plea falls on deaf ears.

# HOW TO WIN AT CARDS

### BY 2NGEL

- **Lesson One: Start Strong.** Everything begins with what is in your hand before a single card hits the table. A good starting hand is the foundation of every win. If you are not holding something worth playing, do not play it. Most players lose not because they play badly, but because they play too often with too little. The best players understand that winning starts long before the first move. It starts with what you choose to hold and what you have the discipline to throw away.

- **Lesson Two: Position, Patience, Aggression.** Three words. Memorize them. Position means acting after your opponent. The player who acts last sees the most. Patience means folding. It means watching round after round go by and refusing to enter until the odds favor you. Most of the money you will ever win will come from the terrible hands you had the discipline to avoid. And aggression means that when you do play, you play hard. A bet puts pressure. A call gives comfort. There is an old saying: play the man, not the cards. That comes later. For now, learn the cards.

- **Lesson Three: Play the Strongest Hand at the Table, Not the Fairest.** Most people at your table are idiots. They are not playing around you. They are playing for fun. You are here to win. There is no prize for creativity. Play what wins. Run what is proven. Your opponent’s choice to ignore optimal play is a confession of weakness, not a display of character.

- **Lesson Four: Learn the Rules.** Every game has rules, and most players learn just enough to play. Winners learn enough to govern. The rules almost always permit a clearly optimal path. Most players never find it because they never looked. Some find it and refuse to walk it because it feels unsporting. That is their problem, not yours.

- **Lesson Five: Control the Clock.** When you are ahead, slow down. Let your opponent sit in the silence of their losing position. Silence does not create clarity for a losing player. It creates dread. When you are behind, play fast. Force mistakes. The player who dictates the pace dictates the outcome.

- **Lesson Six: Choose Your Opponents Before They Choose You.** Never sit down at a table where you are the worst player. Seek out the uncertain, the inexperienced, the emotional. Most people are fragile. Most people are not prepared for someone like you. Once you have found your prey, you are ready to begin.

- **Lesson Seven: Find What They Love and Take It From Them.** Everyone has a card they are proud of. Destroy it. Counter it. Remove it. Do this enough times and something inside them will go quiet. Once they are playing not to lose, they have already lost. You just need to collect.

- **Lesson Eight: Make Them Play Your Game.** A player who is reacting has already surrendered the initiative. Redirect them. Force them into your territory, your timing, your terms. A player trying to beat you is dangerous. A player trying to survive you is already dead.

- **Lesson Nine: Make Them Hate You.** Talk while they think. Compliment their worst decisions. When they make a good play, say nothing. When they make a bad one, smile. They will hate you. They will target you out of spite instead of logic. They will throw away winning positions for the chance to take something from you personally. Good. Anger is the worst strategist who ever lived.

- **Lesson Ten: Winning Is the Only Morality.** There is a sickness in this world that says the game should be fair. This is the lie weak players tell themselves. The winner defines the game. The winner writes the story. If you want to be moral, win. If you want to be right about everything, sit down, shut up, and do not get up from this table until everyone across from you wishes they had never learned how to play.
