The captain begins speaking, asking who we are. [[Otto]] replies that "we are the saviors of the hour," and [[Claire]] finishes: "the Ocean Court Express." The captain seems unfamiliar with us, and says he has no such meeting scheduled—but Kilo jumps in and explains that we're good Samaritans who helped him out when his message was taken.

Kilo gets his reward from the angelic police for his message, and we end up being allowed to meet with this captain for a bit—in return for our aid. We're brought elsewhere to meet. In the elevator, he apologizes to Otto for missing our message to him, offering the explanation that they're overwhelmed with dealing with the rot of this city and it's rare that outsiders come into the city, but he then adds that that's no excuse.

He does check with us whether or not we actually understand the central provision of the city: that we may not be allowed to leave. Claire asks if people manage to leave the city often—he explains that it happens _occasionally_, but through the use of an ancient ritual the angels have control over who can and cannot leave.

The elevator stops and we're brought into a high-tech office space. We're introduced to the messenger angel, Magdalene, who is sat up with perfect posture in front of a computer. He introduces us to her—which makes her perk up and begin apologizing for not passing the message along, but she was going to talk to him during their one-on-one and that got pushed back because he was dealing with Elijah, and—

He cuts her off, saying that the past is the past, and that the future is the only acceptable direction to go. He strides off into his office, and we follow. He has screens up, a conspiracy board, several knick-knacks on his desk. His nametag reads "Officer [[Gabriel]]".

Regarding an offer of help, Gabriel thanks us for the generosity, but the angels' mission to bring order to this place is divinely ordained—and needn't fall onto the shoulders of mortals. Otto asks him if that order _has_ been brought yet, and extolls the virtue and unique point of view that mortals can bring.

Gabriel pauses for a long time, then concedes that Otto is right. Gabriel's been here for 5 years, and in his time here, he has not put an end to the "vile sickness" that lies below. A disease that festers, something _horrid_ that lingers no matter how much they burn the wound. He says he imagines we've seen it.

Gabriel then says he recognizes the crest Otto wears. "You serve the ones from long ago. We fought many good battles with your order." (Unfortunately I had to miss some convo because my computer mouse died -Clio)

Otto expresses his surprise that Gabriel knows who we are. Gabriel explains that he is an angel of Kelemvor, which means he must bring balance between what is above and what festers below. He's a warrior angel—not a messenger, nor representative of systematic changes like blessings and divine intervention. When something must live or die, he is brought into the word. "I'm sure you can see ways in which our paths cross in that."

Otto concedes that we're in the same game but freely admits that there are practical motives—that the aid of law enforcement would help us. Gabriel admits that ulterior motives disappoint him, but he understands that that's simply the purview of mortals, so he asks us to ask away.

Otto says that "ulterior" sounds a bit harsh, and that we do do good work—ultimately, though, he has no specific request. It's just that police backing would do good to our work, which Gabriel should check out on Wildcard at some point. Gabriel says he watches no TV.

This leads into a brief tangent where Claire, [[Squall]], and [[D'Arby]] ask why not and ask what he even listens to. He only listens to police radio.

Gabriel then turns to explaining that there are divine laws he follows—oaths he must uphold, which he would ask we follow when working with him. He asks us to confirm our accordance with this. Otto agrees, D'Arby does a flourish and thumbs-up, Squall agrees then salutes and puts their hand down awkwardly, Claire nods, [[Vayb]] shrugs.

Once he confirms with Otto that that shrug is a confirmation, he asks us to stand in a single file line to receive the blessings. After some uncertainty, we let him begin his incanting and give his blessings.

As he does, someone speaks: "Oh, gods above, is he doing that again?!"

Gabriel acknowledges Elijah, and chastises him for taking the gods' names in vain—Elijah replies that he is literally an angel and therefore CANNOT take their names in vain by definition.

Elijah, another angel, strolls out. Gabriel introduces him to us, says we'll be working with him, and that he "hopes" Elijah will do so with respect. Elijah removes his halo, shifts its form, and seems to... smoke it? Like a cigarette??

Elijah introduces himself and welcomes us to "the closest you'll ever experience to Hell."

Gabriel says we might be able to help the [[AAPD]] with a "difficulty" they've been recently having in the market. Elijah says that that definitely puts us with him—he formally introduces himself and says we're doing a drug bust with him. At one point he says the word "fuck" but it comes out as "fork" like in The Good Place—Squall notices that he seems unused to that and is frustrated by the censorship.

Elijah says he'll give us a briefing, and that he'll take it from here. Before he does, Gabriel does give us one bit of tangible help he can give us. He manifests the sounds of the ground below creaking, and coal rises up. He _crushes_ the coal is his bare hands, and reveals five small diamonds from his closed fist. He explains that these are communication devices that will reach him if we need divine guidance. Otto and Vayb call them rocky-talkies.

Gabriel provides us with one last bit of advice: treat this place as a battlefield. This is a matter of life and death. If we can keep that in mind, we will be just fine.

Elijah cuts him off and sends him to speak to his subordinates—he tells us that Gabriel's a bit intense, as he's one of the angels who volunteered for his. Squall comments that anyone who would volunteer for this is unfit. Elijah counters that Gabriel is _very_ fit. Squall then asks Vayb if they're gonna go to Hell when they die. Vayb says he'd kick any god's ass who sent them there. Elijah tries to cut off the blasphemy there.

Claire asks him about the volunteering thing: yes, most angels here were appointed, rather than it being a choice. We compare it to police probation, like a traffic cop. Elijah concedes it's _something_ like that, but if we think their work is like traffic policing, we have another thing coming.

We spend some time asking Elijah about the nature of the AAPD: who leads them ("the gods"), how that works ("his mission and commands are simply _in his nature_"). Ultimately, though, we just get to the debriefing.

Elijah points out a pair of docks on the map—an area called Necropolis. He explains that, in much the same way that the angels have dominion over the borders of the city, the archdevils have carved out certain areas wherein they can exclude the presence of angels. In those areas, the devils have dominion, and run a protection racket. However, the AAPD scanners have detected _celestial_ energy inside Necropolis. They believe, then, that the devils have managed to get their hands on Angel Wings: an energy inherent to the angels, which they use to fly. However, they suspect the devils have been manipulating the substance and turning it into a drug. Since we are mortals, they reason that we can act freely inside of Necropolis.

He hands us a bag, which Claire takes. Claire hears whispers coming from the bag. She opens it, and inside there are 8 crimson and black coins—they have runes on them, the same as on D'Arby's tattoos. These are _soul coins_, which have major value over there and may (according to Elijah) loosen some lips there.

Otto asks about Mammon's Vault—Eljiah explains that it's run by angels. A solar of Oghma named the Curator is at its head. If we help the AAPD, Otto asks, could that help us secure a meeting with him? Elijah tells us that the angel is a reclusive freak—something about his time here has made him change. Elijah admits they have contact with him, but he really can't guarantee what the Curator would have to say about us asking to meet/them asking him to meet us.

Regarding the Vault's nature, Elijah explains that it's a cooperation between the [[The Sindiath Line|Sindiath Line]] and the angels—funded by the Line to keep cursed items away to "protect their brand", and the angels agree to actually protect the items. It's strange, he admits, that angels need currency, but such is the way that mortals force them to operate.

Otto asks if we're able to get into the Vault—which very much freaks Elijah out. Claire cuts him off and says we have reason to believe there's some sort of a security breach—Otto points out that, if we had malicious intent, it would be deeply foolish to work with the police. Elijah just replies that he hasn't decided yet whether we're smart or stupid—and that either way, we're playing in things bigger than us and outside of what is our responsibilities.

Claire replies that "maybe responsibility isn't the only reason to do something." Otto elaborates on the point that mortals' work can still be useful to them, just like the Vault's deal with the Line—and, in fact, we're kind of an extension of the Line.

At some point in the conversation, someone mentions a "birthday party", a concept that Vayb expresses unfamiliarity with—leading to a big side tangent about how Athasians don't celebrate their birthday and that we need to throw Vayb a birthday party.

Vayb cuts off the tangent by telepathically asking us what, exactly, our plan is here? To achieve our goal?

Otto and Claire point out that we're gathering information and connections as we do this. Claire points out that if [[L1CH]] has connections here, there's clearly more corruption and/or magical loopholes we can find that the angels don't know. But Otto freely admits that we haven't gotten as much as we'd have hoped yet—but we're keeping our eyes open to opportunities.

We're driven to Necropolis by an infernal machine. The streets here, at the Astral Sea's edge, are bathed in neon light and stink of sulfur.

Otto ritual casts _detect magic_, finding magic emanating from an old grey-skinned woman on a rickety raft, from a chrome-decked boat, from a nonchalant tiefling who has his feet up on a desk, and from a hooded figure in front of a set of scrolls.

Vayb strolls up to the tiefling, who asks him "when does the rooster fly?" Vayb takes a puff of his cigarette and asks "what the fuck is a rooster?"

The tiefling is deeply confused by Vayb's lack of knowledge of what a rooster is, despite Vayb's insistence that he wants to buy drugs—the tiefling has no idea how Vayb would know THAT but not know that there was a password. Vayb just says their mutual friend "Kilo" sent him. The tiefling just remarks how CRAZY is it that Kilo has gotten into drugs.

Eventually, he tells Vayb that the password is that "the rooster flies at midnight" if he wants to demonstrate that he's cool. But he doesn't sell drugs himself. However, he _does_ provide help in looking over contracts.

Vayb asks if he knows how to forge credentials that will get us out of the city. The tiefling says that that kind of work would require 11 soul coins. Vayb persuades him down to _4_. But the tiefling "won't promise good work" given how difficult it is, so they argue for a while about how ridiculous that is. Vayb then tries to argue him down to 3 coins, paid only upon completion. Somehow this seems to work...?

In the meantime, D'Arby wanders around the area. He approaches the hooded figure. Who just stares at him for a while. They tilt their head, confused. Eventually, D'Arby lets the telepathic voice through his ring of mind shielding. The voice comments that his mind is shielded. D'Arby admits that a friend of his does telepathy too and he sometimes just wants some peace and quiet.

The figure asks what time the rooster flies. D'Arby just projects an image of a rooster inside the ship, hand-drawn. The figure blinks, then says they can't do business. D'Arby says he didn't know this was a business and that that's a poor way to conduct customer service. The figure and the stall simply _disappear_.

D'Arby keeps talking to him (now in Common Sign language instead of telepathy) and keeps asking questions, until eventually the figure reveals himself again and explains that he sells experiences—as D'Arby keeps asking questions about all of the brain diagrams that are depicted on the chart. Eventually, the figure signs "one moment," walks over to the tiefling and has some kind of telepathic exchange with him.

Meanwhile, Otto is admiring boats.

The figure returns and tells D'Arby that this is a place for "certain business", and that his service is "of memories." You can have something in your brain relived, removed, or even altered. Eventually, D'Arby's conversation with the figure is so confusing and the figure just stops and says "okay, bye." And disappears again.

While this is happening, Claire approaches the old lady. She flips a card and asks "what time does the rooster fly, dearie?" Claire answers with the code (which Vayb had telepathically broadcasted), then critiques how overt their password thing is—the lady simply chuckles and says that they needn't worry about it, under the Clowns' protections.

Claire asks about her business. The lady explains she deals in fate—not just in prediction, but in fate as a mutable thing. Something she can do to you, or to others, through the lens of fate. Claire asks if she has any sort of example or a free sample.

The lady asks Claire to hide a coin behind her back and guesses what hand it's in. She does it once. Twice. A third time. All correct. But the fourth time, when she guesses "wrong", she has Claire reveal that there is, in fact, a coin in that hand.

Claire asks how—and the woman explains that she took hold of and strangled the strings of fate.

"The devil's dog sells the angel's feather in hiding. The one that puts up the ward is the one that hides in a secret world. The gateway to his place is small, but through his chrome arm and wired eyes, you might have access."

Claire comments she shouldn't be surprised that that was cryptic. The lady replies that it _was_ a fortune. But, anyway, given Claire's goal, she suspects that she's not just looking for a guilt high. Claire asks and the lady explains some of Angel Wing's effects: it, essentially, clears your conscience. Makes your mind free of sin within yourself.

As Claire starts to say goodbye, there is a ROAR that echoes over the region—an engine to the right, which begins to fill the area with smoke with its burn. Everyone in the area disappears, running and hiding as silhouettes ride in.

A gang of ape-like demons roll in on motorcycles, blowing past people without any care or consideration. They corner the chrome boat and the individual selling from there. The demons' faces are painted bright white with black teardrops painted onto their lower cheeks.
