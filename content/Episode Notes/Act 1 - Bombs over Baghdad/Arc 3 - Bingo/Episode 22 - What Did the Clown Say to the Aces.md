A bike careens into the lake, and shortly after landing in the water, there is a "BOOM!", spraying a cool mist up into the air.

The fox man—an arcanaloth—that we saved clambers back a bit and looks at us. Then knocks on the wall of the invisible box the mimes placed him in.

[[Claire]] glances at the party to decide what to do. [[Vayb]] makes telepathic comment, and the fox man curls into the fetal position because of the "voices coming back." But then he makes the connection and realizes it's Vayb doing some "telepathy bullshit."

He thanks us for saving him—"very much appreciate that"—but he is stuck in a box. Claire sighs and begins meticulously ritual casting _detect magic_. [[D'Arby]] asks what she's doing and we aren't keen on quite how long this will take. Claire tells D'Arby to try something if he has a better idea. Vayb pulls out a crowbar to hand to him, and the arcanaloth is VERY freaked about us trying to smash it.

Then, D'Arby has a _much_ better idea. He reaches his hand out, searching for a doorknob and mimes opening up the door. When he faces some resistance, Vayb mimes handing him a key. And suddenly, the seal of the barrier breaks. By miming it.

The arcanaloth strolls out and thanks us again, verbally this time, and offers to answer questions.

Vayb asks him what a clown is. The arcanaloth replies that "you must be new here, huh?"

He asks Vayb to do a twirl, to make sure he's not an angel—and concludes that we're just noobs. He tells us that the clowns are the new demon faction, getting new ranks every day. They're forcing him to pay for a protection racket to them, as well as to the devils. The devils were supposed to help him in situations like this, anyway, and they aren't here!

As for what he does, the fox man does _body modifications_—prosthetics, special eyes, gatling guns, etc. Vayb is VERY interested in this business, and gets the arcanaloth's phone number to do business with.

Claire, finally, asks for his name: Ziozibar.

D'Arby reflects on the infernal teaching he got from [[Otto]]: this guy is a yugoloth, a crafty and neutral type of fiend from the Lower Planes.

Ziozibar throws the phone back to Vayb and gets back into his boat—a dingy piece of crap, packed with a pair of incredibly old computers and a plethora of different pieces of machinery, prostheses, and his other modifications. Ziozibar tells "his babies" that he's so happy they're okay and has no idea what he would have done if the mimes started taking things.

He tells us to take a seat.

He explains that this stuff can be pretty intense and for bios like us he's probably gonna want to limit us to three modifications. And, the next thing to know is that this tech isn't [[The Sindiath Line|Sindiath Line]] quality—and beyond worrying about it breaking down, this is a very good thing. You don't have to worry about any of their updates or being tracked or anything like that. Which is very important to his customers, of course.

He directs Vayb to look at his website.

While this happens, D'Arby tells Claire that this seems very sketchy, which she agrees is VERY obvious. As for whether we should stop Vayb, Claire just shrugs and says he can do whatever he wants.

Meanwhile, Otto's been investigating the bikes outside, and considering his knowledge about infernals. For one, the one barlgura summoning another used a teleportation circle that is fairly low-range: able to summon only from within the city. Additionally, Otto has a good sense of how _long_ fiends have been on the Material Plane—and most of them demons struck him as fairly fresh, recent arrivals to this plane. Otto then turns back to see what important stuff the others have been doing.

He arrives to find D'Arby smoking outside the ship and doing nothing of import.

Otto greets him and says that was a nice move with the miming: "did you learn that at school for amnesiac samurai?" Otto asks if they've learned anything yet, and D'Arby explains that the fox guy is trying to upsell Vayb on body mods.

We cut back. Vayb is squinting, trying to make sense of the symbols. [[Squall]] starts reading it aloud, and Vayb starts sagely nodding along and "discussing" it with them. (He can't read.)

Vayb points to the Data Lock—which allows him to secret away memories. Ziozibar says that's a tougher one, since it's a brain modification, but offers to set him up. Squall points out that his body is relatively fungible, but his mind is very important—they'd say more, but the fox man is still here. Ziozibar says he'll leave them to some privacy. So they initiate telepathy, and Squall asks if he's worried about harming his mind. He escaped "our modifications" with his mind intact. "Why play that game again?"

Vayb just says he thinks it would be good for him to forget some things—but they'd still be there if he needed them. He just wouldn't have to think of them most of the time. Squall says this problem could have other solutions. They've heard of something on other planets called a "theerapist" that can help with things like this.

They tell him that his mind is his own to do with as he sees fit, but as someone who has only the one mind, they fear he couldn't _u&#x6E;_&#x64;o this if the worst occurs. Vayb says he just figures his mind is broken as is. It's like gamble. Squall objects to Vayb's appeal to their love of gambling.

Squall concedes that they won't stop him from making reckless decisions, but they want to make sure he knows not to play a game if he doesn't know the stakes.

Meanwhile, Claire pokes around at the arcanaloth's workshop looking for drugs, but comes up empty-handed. She finds the pieces for a _dopamine booster_ and turns it over in her hands, staring at it. At that point, the communicator goes off and Elijah checks in. Claire tells him we're working on it, but we need to put him away until Ziozibar comes back.

Meanwhile, Ziozibar left and ran into Otto. The two talk and then Otto gets to asking why he was attacked—and Ziozibar says he didn't pay the demon racket, both because of his principles and because he would have come up short.

Otto suggests that we may be able to remove the threats. Ziozibar stops him and says that he's not gonna pay a THIRD group for protection! And the clowns are some of his best customers. Otto says we're not interested in his money, and asks if he looks like someone who'd make a careless suggestion.

Otto says he doesn't want him to feel like he's pulled into another transaction, but that there are a lot of bad creatures and people in this city, and he'd like to lower that number a little bit. Like with the mimes.

Ziozibar explains that they're new and, from what he's heard, they're holed up in the old theater uptown. As far as how they've gotten this strong it's not that (as Otto suggests) they're filling a void, it's that more seem to keep coming when they get killed. "They're like a fuckin' hydra, man!"

The two continue conversing, and end up walking in to rejoin the others. Ziozibar asks if we've been stealing anything—Claire just snaps out of her focus on the dopamine booster and says "huh? what?" Ziozibar says that it seems like we're fine, he guesses.

Otto introduces his idea: that Ziozibar could help us break into Mammon's Vault. As for why we would want Ziozibar, Otto says that we've been stonewalled on getting into the Vault, and that his skillset could expand our options.

Ziozibar explains that Mammon's Vault is _fully_ sanctioned by the [[AAPD]] and the Curator, with a very tight-knit group. He's got a message boy, and lots of automatons, which is the extent of what Ziozibar knows about his group—except that the Curator never leaves the Vault. Maybe getting an in with the AAPD could help.

Otto thinks about the options for a second, but he "agrees" with Ziozibar's suggestion. We're not interested, he says, in really helping the AAPD, but if we had an in with them it would help our goal. And he heard that the angels were looking for a lead on "angel dust"—if we had a lead for them on that, maybe they'd help.

Ziozibar says that snitching for information seems like a risky gambit. Otto says it's probably the kind of thing we could say we dug up after an encounter with some mimes. His intensity pushes Ziozibar to explain that angel dust is devil work. Otto asks if he has any.

"What, you think just because I'm some quack surgeon I have lots of drugs?"

A beat.

"Because you're right."

He tosses a packet to Otto. But tells him that this is, like. Something we owe him a bit for.

Before we leave with that information, Vayb asks him if he can get that cyberware installed, and then asks about the risks. Ziozibar explains that it's the same sort of risk you get with any computer. But it's in your brain.

Otto goes to leave, and tells Vayb not to "do anything I wouldn't do".

The DM and player disappear into a private voice channel to do the surgery.

During this time, Claire asks Squall if they think these augments would be a good idea. Squall very much cautions affecting the mind—they explain that a humanoid mind and personhood is something _special_ that is not worth messing with—but they don't dislike body modifications, and they can understand wanting a body more like theirs. They are very much worried about Vayb's mind being affected by Ziozibar's technology, in any case.

Otto, for his part, thinks that it smacks too much of borrowed power, something he's leery of. Perhaps it's judgmental of him, he says, but it feels like a shortcut—power gained in a place like this requires giving something in return.

"So, what?" Claire asks. "We just let [[2NGEL]] win?" Otto replies that we'll win, we'll just do it as ourselves. Claire leaves, frustrated. She bites down a comment, and sits on the dock.

Squall moves to follow her, but Otto stops them. He asks them a genuine question: "Have you and Vayb ever dealt with the illithid?"

Just at that moment, Ziozibar returns and tells everyone that Vayb chickened out of his operation, so we're free to go. However, Claire interrupts and says that she's ready to go.

Claire is sat down on the table, and provides Ziozibar with a list: a datajack, three augments, and one cyberware program. Ziozibar reminds her of the risks and reminds her to avoid being knocked out—"it's easier to fuck with you when there are wires in your head".

Meanwhile, the rest of the party asks Vayb why he had a change of heart. "Change of _mind_", he replies. Otto asks whether the reasoning was internal or external. Vayb shrugs. "Basically internal." Otto says that's sufficient, he just doesn't want us to be at risk if it's unnecessary.

We cut. Claire wakes up in a pool of her own blood. An outlet plug sits in the back of her neck for her skillsoft and for data transfer. Wires between the clear exterior and the whites of her eyes glow a faint pink, neon light.

Before we leave, Ziozibar gives Otto a contact with the devils, a lead on the ward and the angel dust.

Then, we depart Ziozibar's boat. Ziozibar asks if we wanna do a blood circle later. Vayb replies that he's SO in, and that Ziozibar has his number.

Otto calls Elijah once we've left, informing him of our progress. He also checks in again about whether we'll actually get our in with the Curator in return. Elijah sighs, and levels with us—saying that we're doing good work and that, as mortals, he's sure we'll cause problems if they don't reward us for our work. He says that what he's able to do might be a bit dubious, but he'll do what he can to connect us with the Curator. He'll give us his word.

We end the call and begin discussing our game plan now: sneaky, guns blazing, where to start, etc.

We start with Ziozibar's contact! We walk through misty streets toward the given address, a _thick_ fog nearly blocking off sight. But it clears, pulls back, and reveals a ratty brick building with a sign of a clown on it and a name: The Gilded Cage. The door opens before we even get close enough to touch the doorknob.

A ramshackle gambling den is set up here: poker tables, roulette tables, old slot machines. But the place is nearly _empty_, and is nearly pitch black. But, there is a green box with an LED screen sitting on the bar.

D'Arby spots security cameras watching us, and waves.

We approach the box, and the screen begins flashing red. It beeps. Beep! Beep! Beep! Beepbeepbeepbeepbeepbeep!

The rest of us pull back and Otto raises his and braces against it. A banana cream pie splatters against his shield, and laughing erupts from the darkness.

> whatever else can be said, WOW do i respect these guys for their commitment to the bit

"I am sorry about that, folks!" The voice explains that they wanted to give us the real clown experience, when they heard there was gonna be newcomers. A person _swings_ down the hall toward us, dangling from wires—and reveals themself as a tiefling.

Otto swipes some of the pie filling off his shield and tastes it. It's actually delicious!

The tiefling has curling ram horns, a metallic grin, and a completely calm demeanour. He adjusts a tie on his yellow suit and makes sure his clown make-up is ready.

We begin a meeting with this fellow, a well-to-do member of the clowns. (Vayb nods in understanding, finally learning what a clown is) His name is [[Bingo]].

While we meet, to assure his safety, Bingo claps his hands and several red dots settle on our chests and/or heads. After all, Bingo doesn't negotiate if he doesn't have the upper hand. Otto grimaces and says that this being "the upper hand" to him really shows how much he needs our help.

He says that he's heard through the grapevine that we're quite talented, but he has his drones training modified antimatter rifles on us—maybe we'd survive, maybe we won't, but probably all of us don't want to find out. And if we're here to do business in friendship? Well, we have nothing to worry about!

Otto says that if our intention was violent, we wouldn't have even gotten this far.

Bingo admires the moxie, and says that if we're here to talk, we'll talk. "Dazzle me."

Otto explains that we're [[Aces]], here to get into the Vault of Mammon. It's important that this happens, he explains, for the safety of more people than Bingo would be able to comprehend. Bingo raises his eyebrows and says that that's ambitious, to break into what is called the "Unbreakable Wall of Heaven."

Otto says that it's less that we're breaking in, and more that we'll walk in, invited. If we give information to the AAPD, we'll get their favour.

Bingo asks what good this does him. Otto says that the tips the AAPD gets might directly screw over the Clowns' enemies. And, potentially, something else—something Bingo wants—might leave the Vault.

Otto explains that the AAPD is closer to getting information than Bingo might think—and if he can control the flow of information, he can control WHAT the AAPD actually gets.

Bingo says that he's not sure the Man Down Below would necessarily enjoy him going out on a limb like this.

We suggest that maybe we should be talking to Him instead—Otto rises, saying that maybe Bingo doesn't have what we need.

Bingo tells us to slow our roll and discusses more earnestly. In conclusion: Bingo needs a little skin in the game to trust us to make this deal. While we grab what we need, we let Bingo in, and then we go our separate ways.

We confer telepathically and agree that this is probably our best bet—whether we decide to double cross Bingo later or not. We agree to make the deal with Bingo, and he gives us information.

Below the illithid's stand in Necropolis, he has a lab. In that lab, he cooks all day and all night long, very excited. As for where he gets the feathers, Bingo doesn't really sniff where he doesn't belong... but it seems obvious. The angel wings come from a man on the inside. Angels die. And, well, if those bodies find their way here? Well, we got business!

Then we ask about the ward on this district. Bingo brags that the work is the result of "yours truly!" Or, well, he can't take all the credit. The arcane theory is his work, but he is a goon at the end of the day. He does what he's told. And he's been customized to do so: ripped together, torn apart, and improved, thanks to the handiwork of our mutual contact.

Bingo tells us that he'll show us the way out: he moves his hand, pulls out a phone, and presses buttons. Then a glowing door appears.

He asks if there's anything else we need. D'Arby asks if Bingo knows any jokes. Bingo tells him a joke about paladins, leaving D'Arby positively whelmed. Claire and Squall engage with the puns a bit, though.

We leave into the glowing door, and reappear in an open grassy field. The wind blows gently. The sun shines.

A ball is thrown through the door after us. No, not a ball. A red clown nose. And the door shuts behind us.

Bingo's voice projects. "I'm sorry, folks. I don't mean to be rude, but you're sniffing around in territory you shouldn't be sniffing after. I was warned about a group that may be looking for this Codex, but I didn't expect you to come right to me."

He tells D'Arby a joke, and the door shuts down, leaving us trapped in a demiplane.
