The episode begins with the boys chatting at the Laughing Beholder. They're chatting about the bomb we have on the ship, realizing we... have not really addressed it. [[D'Arby]] told us about how he'd made some sort of a deal or friendship with Civlar, who has experience with the bomb. [[Otto]] insists that we've gotta get rid of the bomb before our next mission, no matter what. They started discussing some other place where we could hide it.

Moving on from this topic after a brief interaction with [[PIXIE|Pixie]] (who thought they wanted to blow up the bomb, for whatever reason), Otto asks D'Arby about where he learned to fight, which dovetails into a conversation about how [[Vayb]] learned magic from monks, and that his psionic magic is really unique and interesting—Otto has no experience with magic coming from the mind like that.

Their bonding sesh continues for a little while longer. (Notetaker had to go switch laundry, sorry for lack of specificity) At one point during the bonding, they decide they're the Three Musketeers—which makes [[Squall]] and [[Claire]] the Lesser Musketeers.

Squall ambles up to the Laughing Beholder shortly before Wildcard starts—Vayb orders them a drink. [[Large Luigi]] says he thinks it's odd to be making a drink for someone who can't drink. He's totally willing to make a robot equivalent if they want him to!

D'Arby asks if they have Purple Worm rings—Luigi responds by asking "do beholders kill their dads at age 2?" Apparently this is a beholder tradition.

Soon enough, Claire arrives too and Wildcard starts!

Live from Black's Jack—it is Wildcard, with our host Jack! After a week missing us, he'd glad to be back in the studio. He tells us of some events in the galaxy on the war front (note to self: get this in detail from the DM later).

Today's bounty comes from Elvish High Command. Some of our greatest enemies wear familiar guises. Surveillance shows that General Shaw's ship fled in the middle of battle, deserting and betraying his nation. He's been tracked to The Cluster. The bounty clocks in at 7750 gold pieces!

> \[!Claire Note]
> wow, a major elf fugitive already. this is interestinggg

The group starts chatting about the bounty and thoughts on the matter—wondering about the fact that our last couple bounties have been war deserters. Luigi chimes in and points out that these bounties are Sindiath-funded, and, well... the galaxy's in wartime right now. It's a significant thing. Otto asks Luigi for some context—space wasn't always so civilized. Once, it was much wilder—and pirates were abound. The Elvish Navy, however, organized and launched the Unhuman War (not called the First Unhuman War at the time), driving the pirates out to Darkspace.

A pirate named Dukagsh united the pirates in return, under the banner of what is called The Scro—and they've instigated a second Unhuman War, to get revenge on the elves. And that's the gist of the war that has been going on for the past few years.

Luigi goes to offer us a tip—to which Vayb grins and says, "you sly dog, you _like_ us!" Luigi sputters, pointing out that he _is_ our sponsor. He asks D'Arby if he's gotten used to the new middle name yet. D'Arby only blinks in confusion. The tip is a shortcut, likely to drain magic on the way, but he knows of a font where we can recharge.

We move on from this, discussing what our plan of action was—then what to do about the bomb. Apparently, part of the concern is that apparently technically Civlar still has remote access. D'Arby trusts Civlar, but Vayb doesn't trust the situation—hard to say if Civlar even really knew how to keep ahold of it, how not to lose control, etc. D'Arby concedes that Vayb makes sense.

The party discusses splitting up—the Boys are going to the Garden to look up on the World Wide Weave. Claire and Squall will look into the bomb and Claire might see if she can pull any Elvish contacts.

The boys roll up to the Garden, which is silent, but then they hear the sounds of alien shrieking. Dr. Hickory says "one second!" then appears a few seconds later, holding roots that seem to be actively bleeding human blood. He says he'll let them use the computer.

At the end of the conversation, he tells them that if they see any seeds that whisper that they need to grow inside their bones... to ignore them, and then let Hickory know.

They set up on the computer, and begin trying to research [[Shaw|General Shaw]].

Actually, never mind, Vayb and D'Arby get distracted by trying to round up these seeds apparently. If they do, Hickory will do some research for them himself. Otto thinks this is ridiculous and is gonna go do research himself.

Otto looks on forums to try to find out information on General Shaw.

- To start with, he looks into the Cluster. It's a mining community that has been established in the far reaches of known Flow. One of the more populated areas within the [[Shilear]] Cluster, is the town Shilear on the asteroid Shilear.
- Reports of where Shaw's ship went suggest that he's in the town of Shilear specifically. He's been in that town specifically, since he deserted.
- Reports about the battle suggest that his performance in the battle was really clumsy and suboptimal—perhaps that desertion was an impulse decision (Notetaker's note: leaving for honour?)
- Shilear produces a valuable mineral. Lately, pirates have been taking advantage of this. A fleet called the Void (missed this word) have been raiding the town for as much as they can take.

Otto comes out of this perfectly willing to take out this General—at the very least, for his cowardice.

Meanwhile, D'Arby sees a seed with a tiny little sprout that starts psychically speaking to him in a squeaky lil voice. D'Arby approaches, kneels, and introduces himself. It says hi and that he must have questions. He says that he has fewer questions than it probably expects. It says that's good, then—it knows what he needs. "Therapy?", he replies. It says no; he needs it to grow inside of his bones.

Vayb approaches, and D'Arby tells him he found one. They talk about getting Hickory, and the seed starts begging for him to not get Hickory, and about how much it _needs_ the bones. Hickory scoops it up in an anti-psychic jar. D'Arby looks at Vayb, and D'Arby seems legitimately a little haunted. Vayb goes to check on Otto, and D'Arby almost gets got by another seed before Hickory saves him.

Meanwhile, Squall and Claire head to the Ocean Court to go check out the bomb.

The hippo looms over Squall, seemingly almost begging to blow up some tarrasques. Squall jacks in to the bomb. Squall investigated it for how Civlar used the bomb to talk to D'Arby—best they can tell, it wasn't a phone, but just the end point for a divination ritual he'd performed. Additionally, Squall investigated the remote hacking by [[L1CH]].

Essentially, threadweavers can hack into remote systems they didn't make, but L1CH was able to move more smoothly through the threads. Based on Squall's rudimentary knowledge of hacking—one explanation for L1CH's ease with hacking is pre-existing knowledge of its data. They already knew the password, so to speak. The metadata shows that [[Marshal Plimbitt|Plimbitt]] didn't create the bomb—the creator uses a callsign of their own: [[2NGEL]]. L1CH was given the access they had by this 2NGEL, presumably.

> \[!Claire Note]
> so... L1CH is one, 2NGEL is two, huh? strange.

Squall tries to change the password/make the bomb inaccessible as best they can. They succeed at wresting control from the programs that were already in place.

Meanwhile, Claire sets up a call with her father: [[Maxim G. Ainsworth]]. She's left on hold for a long time—but eventually a secretary picks up. The secretary asks if she's begging for money again, and she annoyedly points out that she's an Ace now, so no. She wants information.

When he father picks up, he greets her and asks if she's still into that "horse princess" show. She sighs and reminds him that that was 17 years ago. Onto business, he insists that she give him a short elevator pitch, like how they've always practiced. She pitches wrapping up this problem for the elves and to benefit her group. They discuss what history can be gleaned about General Shaw—notably, he's apparently managed to _completely wipe all records of his past_. He's a very, _very_ old elf, too, who has lots of skills in war and charisma. Likely, he's been (Maxim shudders about it)... purchasing _locally_, and as a skilled statesman, it's entirely plausible that he's been amassing influence (a militia or army, perhaps?) locally. (As notetaker: there are pirates causing problems here. Maybe he's opportunistically creating loyalty by stopping the pirate problems)

> \[!Claire Note]
> they really have no idea what i'm up to, huh. sheesh...

The group gathers, shares their information, and then set off into the stars. Otto trains with [[Darrett]] for a while, and the group comes to watch—Claire videos, D'Arby works out, and Otto keeps kicking Darrett's ass since Darrett won't stop announcing his attacks.

Pixie chimes in along the journey, informing us that Vayb's energy is close to running out (as expected), but the Astral Font is nearby, so we can stop there—but she's detecting two entities there. She starts to explain, but Otto asks for video.

In this place, there is a basin with water—the water seeming more like _light_ than liquid. The land floats, suspended, not quite in the Flow, not quite out of it. Like it's suspended in time. Two _gigantic_ individuals lurk over basin. One figure is of pure geometric perfection: diamond. The other figure is random and chaotic, shifting in energy.

Pixie explains that these entities seem to be _divine_. Gods, manifesting here above the Astral Font. We debate what to do, as this is particularly distressing to Otto—but we need to stop by anyway, so we get closer.

As we get closer, the beings are in what seems to be an argument. The diamond one says that these things are set in stone, that the other's ideas are an insult to the Tome of Fate. The second says that it intends to _honour_ the Tome of Fate—that this is why they're here, what they exist for.

The moment our ship would hit the Astral Font, the ship suddenly freezes. The gods notice the mortals, and they ask why we've come.

They don't speak in any particular language. A language none of us have heard, but one that we understand anyway. Their voices boom through space.

Vayb tells them that we are just passing through. D'Arby asks Otto what's happening.

The flowing one asks why the mortal (Vayb) is speaking so casually. The hard one says that the mortals have no idea what they're doing.

Otto does a warding gesture, then speaks to the gods, begging forgiveness.

They seem to calm by this, and the hard one asks if we intend to drink from the Astral Font. Vayb says he's the only one who wants to. The gods reply that he can't—that they're working on this piece of creation and are debating on establishing canon. But we can come back in a couple thousand years when they're done.

Vayb replies that he's on a time limit. Otto hisses at Vayb to be respectful.

D'Arby apologizes to the "two big cool dudes" and explains that we need to keep moving and we're on a time limit. He asks how they stopped the ship, and that it's kinda scary. They reply that it was very easy. He asks if they can make us go faster.

They explain that they preside only over a certain area of creation, called the Four Lights.

D'Arby interrupts them, asking if they could do that in _their_ space. A zipper opens up a portal, and D'Arby is promptly sent to a demiplane.

They introduce their names—minor gods of (the hard one) knick-knacks, paddywhacks, and bones; and (the soft one) of awkward but still consensual physical intimacy. But now, in this space, they are expressing themselves as gods of order and intention.

Squall, seeing D'Arby get pocket dimensioned, almost goes for their gun and is stopped by Otto in time.

Claire speaks up and (in the spirit of an elf who respects her progenitors) wishes to follow her father's maxims—the three Bs: benefits for you, benefits for me, business to do.

Before they can respond to this, Vayb "backs her up", explaining that they can also release our friend because they did something fucked up and—he goes on. He gets pocket dimensioned. Squall panics and draws their gun. Claire casts _suggestion_ to them to "stop."

> \[!Claire Note]
> are... are otto and i the only ones with common sense? T\_T

> \[!Squall Note]
> This was not panic. I was going to kill those things.

Finally listening to Otto and Claire, the gods concede to let the pocketed characters go.

Meanwhile, D'Arby has fallen a long time into a MASSIVE, nefarious labyrinth: a marketplace. Hundreds of merchants chase him around, hunting him around like dogs, flashing trinkets, desperately trying to get his gold in a crazy wave of overstimulation. D'Arby freaks out and leaps into the sky with his _ring of jumping_.

Meanwhile, Vayb is on the third date with this woman that he's absolutely smitten with. His hand brushes hers and the gesture has MEANING. Is it a move? Is it big? Small? What could it mean? And then he notices, that oh my god his hand is really sweaty, when did that happen? He plays off a gesture and runs his hand through his hair. He awkwardly compliments the movie and the girl seems awkward about the interaction. He asks if she needs water. She doesn't want anything to drink. He sits back down and puts his hands in his lap, then glances at her. He puts his hand down on the couch beside her, hand with his palm open for her to make the move. She decides she _does_ want water, then, and gets up.

A zipper opens up in each dimension and they allow D'Arby and Vayb to leave.

They ask Claire about the proposition, and she gasses up our party—saying we could maybe help them as powerful, well-traveled mortals. They ask if, perhaps, we could offer moral advice. Claire blanches and defers to Otto. The gods explain that the Tome of Fate, a tome of rules by which they make all their decisions. The flowing one explains that the crystal that creates the Astral Water is noted as magical, but the water itself isn't noted as magical. So, they debate on whether or not conjuration wizards can or cannot replicate this water with their magical skills. The gods begin debating Rules as Written, and Rules as Intended.

Meanwhile, Claire sees that Squall is still frozen and "stopped", so she drops concentration. Squall's brain revs up. They go on a long speech about how they can't prove they're really gods, or at least major gods and essentially that they ask major gods for a ruling. The gods ask everyone else to weigh in.

Otto just mutters that "we're all gonna die."

Claire says a weird simile about how the water is infused with magic, so to summon nonmagical items wouldn't mean you could summon an infused item—much like how you can't make coffee with no beans.

Vayb says he just backs up Squall.

Which leaves us with D'Arby. D'Arby just feels afraid and uncomfortable, so Claire urges him to just back her up. He asks if that would get him sent to the hell dimension. She says it only will if he's mean.

Which brings us back to Otto... who quietly gets up. He walks to the gods, thinking of how to phrase things.

Meanwhile, Squall debates with Claire—Vayb hangs by Squall, and D'Arby sits by Claire who pats his head like a scared puppy.

Otto says that, as mortals, we cannot speak to the specifics of divine rules. All we can do is guess at their intention, and he backs up Claire as being in line with what things he thought were intended.

The gods concede that they got a bit... overzealous about the tome and their rulings. They thank us for giving them perspective, relent, and depart. They allow us to recharge Vayb and take a dose of the water for ourselves as we leave.

And as the Ocean Court takes off into space once more... we roll credits!
