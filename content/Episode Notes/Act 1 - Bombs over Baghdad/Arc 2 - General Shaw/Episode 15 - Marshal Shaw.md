The train (which [[Claire]] brought for us?) creaks along. The high noon sun casts a bright glow over [[Shilear]].

We pivot to catching Claire up on what happened, while Vayb leaves to go try to pull the whistle. [[Otto]] explains the meeting with Blackeye, with [[2NGEL]], and our equivocation on the deal he offered, and discuss what to do next.

Otto, looking outside, sees a sleek spaceship—a sleek microjammer—falling out of the sky and into the desert. Otto points it out to Vayb, Claire casts _fly_ and flies over to go see what's going on.

The ship crashes into the desert, smoke billowing from the wreckage. With a metallic screech, the cockpit explodes outwards, something pushing out. A silhouette pushes himself out—an astral elf with pale green skin, marked with cuts, blood trickling from his temple. He moves with a limp, scanning the area.

Shaw calls out for Kyra. Someone is banging in the cockpit. Otto leaps into action to break open the cockpit. Claire pulls out her spellbook and points a hand toward him—not ready to cast a spell, her metaphorical finger is not on the trigger, but she has her gun ready.

Shaw asks where we are—how far we are away from town. Seven miles, is the correct answer.

Nearby, sand begins to cascade as something moves toward us, beneath the desert sand. Something purple _surges_ out of the desert, moving toward us at breakneck speeds. Smoke billows from the engine—it will explode soon.

Shaw tells us that what we're doing is _profoundly_ stupid, but if we're gonna do this, he has a suggestion—leading it back toward the shallow canyon, the Chode. The others head off, but Claire still needs to concentrate on her _fly_, so she heads back to the train car and checks on Kyra.

The rest of the team gangs up on the wurm and corrals it toward the Chode.

Vayb gave the group a telepathic comment about how it's odd that he cares so much about leaving people behind.

He asks if we're serious about working to help this place. Otto agrees that we wanna help "this place". Shaw says that that's antithetical to the main idea of being Aces—"gambling with people's lives". Otto says he wasn't always an Ace, and has history working in the service of others.

Shaw says we seem like the kind of people who make deals—and that we've proven a certain amount of competence. He tells us that there are "certain things you have to know about all this". But that he's not ignorant enough to not know that there's a penalty to be had for what he's done.

He says this: "You help me deal with these people. You help me _protect_ this town. ... I turn myself in."

Otto questions this sudden willingness to work with the first people who come to him, after all he's done to desert. Claire points out that we're "pretty exceptional". Vayb senses a significant dread in him, but that he seems earnest. And that he's willing to be honest.

Vayb asks why he deserted. He stammers that he didn't _desert_ exactly—and asks what we were told. We reply that they said he deserted.

Shaw says that's not what happened. Not at all.

There was a big battle, one he and his crew had prepared for a while. He tells us about their base, the gas they spewed that enhanced their spellcasting. He tells us of their charge, and how the gas didn't enhance them. Nothing happened to their magic, but instead everyone went mad. Enemy and ally blurred, formations fell apart, something _infected_ them, caused them to see everyone—ever&#x79;_&#x74;hing_—as an enemy. He watched his lieutenant, an honest man that he'd served for YEARS with, tear into a wounded ally with his bare hands.

He tells us how it wasn't mind control per se. He remembers feeling in control, that he _wanted_ to do it, only that NOW he thinks about it and wants to vomit. He remembers ending up at the end amidst a field of bodies.

The Elvish Armada had given him something (that tablet) to him, and with no idea what to do he fled—and he gives us a speech about how that pad has the data and history of what happened on it, that there's something deeper going on and that we can't let it get into their hands and how we HAVE to believe him.
