# What is Threadweaving

In the age of the World Wide Weave, magic has become standardized, connected, and more powerful than ever before. But with this standardization came vulnerability. Enter the art of Threadweaving - a revolutionary discipline that allows skilled practitioners to interface directly with magical networks, manipulating the very fabric of modern magical infrastructure.

As a threadweaver, you'll navigate digital landscapes, battle security systems, and vie for control of crucial magical nodes. Your physical form remains vulnerable while your mind traverses the Weave, making teamwork and timing essential to survival.

> [!warning] Prerequisites
> To practice the art of threadweaving, a character must:
>
> - Be a spellcaster
> - Have proficiency with Hacking Tools

# Understanding The Digital Landscape

## Jacking In

The first step in any threadweaving operation is establishing a connection with a Weave System. To _**"jack in"**_ to any given system, you must be within melee range of the target system. When in range, players are able to plug in their USB. Each Weave System has its own "Arcane Charge" to it, as stated by your USB. This charge essentially lays out the spell slot required to jack into a certain system. If a player expends the designated spell slot to charge their USB, they are able to Jack In.

Upon connection, your consciousness enters the digital space while your physical body becomes _**incapacitated**_ with a speed of 0. Though you cannot take physical actions, you can still communicate verbally with your allies.

> [!danger] Physical Vulnerability
> While your mind navigates the Weave System, your physical body remains vulnerable to attack. You retain awareness of your surroundings but cannot take physical actions until you jack out. Coordination with your party members is crucial - they must protect your paralyzed form during the incursion. Once a party member has jacked on, they have the ability to be moved by party members using standard carrying capacity rules.
>
> Any damage dealt to your physical body affects you normally. You can safely disconnect from a Weave System by returning to an entry point and using your action to jack out. Alternatively, you can attempt an emergency disconnect from any node by using your action and making a Hacking Tools check (DC determined by the DM based on system complexity). However, if you're reduced to 0 hit points while jacked in, you're immediately forced to jack out and fall unconscious. Like any character at 0 hit points, you must then make death saving throws and can be stabilized or healed by your allies.

## The Digital Landscape

Once jacked in, you perceive both the physical world and the Weave System simultaneously. The digital space manifests as a network of interconnected nodes and pathways overlaid upon reality - a parallel dimension of pure magical data.

### Node Types

When jacked into a Weave System, you'll find yourself navigating through interconnected nodes - distinct "spaces" within the magical architecture of a weave system. Think of it like rooms in a traditional dungeon. Each node is a stable position from which you can observe and interact with the system, connected to other nodes by glowing pathways of magical thread.

On your turn, you can move through these nodes, traveling a number of spaces equal to your Intelligence modifier. For example, a threadweaver with 16 Intelligence (+3 modifier) could move through up to three nodes on their turn. Like exploring a dungeon, you'll need to choose your path carefully - some routes may be guarded by Firewards, while others might lead to valuable treasure nodes containing critical system controls.

<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>

Within any given Magical System, there are:

- **Entry Points** mark safe connection and disconnection locations within the network. You'll always begin your incursion at one of these points, and can safely jack out by returning to them.
- **Treasure Nodes** represent critical system functions - security cameras, communication systems, automated defenses, and more. These are your primary targets, offering direct control over real-world systems.
- **Empty Nodes** serve as waypoints and strategic positions in the network. While they don't provide direct control over systems, they're crucial for navigation and tactical positioning.

# Taking Action

When you jack into a Weave System, you operate in turns just like physical combat. If your party is already in combat, you'll act on your normal initiative. If not, turn-based sequence begins when you jack in, with Firewards and rival threadweavers acting in their own turns.

On your turn, you can take one of the following actions:

- Claim a new node
- Shut down a claimed node
- Control a claimed node
- Jack out (free at an entry point; requires a Hacking Tools check elsewhere)

## Claiming Nodes

When you reach a treasure node, you can use your action to claim it. This establishes your connection to the node's systems, allowing you to interact with its functions through the next two actions.

![[Images/Nodes two(1).png| center | 570]]

## Shutting Down Nodes

You can use your action to completely deactivate a claimed node's functions. This essentially makes the features and abilities of the node null and void. For example, if your party is hacking a robot and a threadweaver successfully hacks and shuts down the weapon system, the robot is no longer able to use their ranged attacks. This shutdown persists even after you jack out, making it an effective way to permanently disable security systems. A shutdown node remains inactive until another threadweaver claims it.

## Controlling Nodes

Active control offers more sophisticated manipulation of a claimed node's functions. While controlling a node, you might redirect security cameras to create blind spots, selectively trigger alarm systems to misdirect guards, or even reprogram automated defenses to target your enemies. However, this requires you to remain on the node.

> [!important] Maintaining Control
> When maintaining active control of a treasure node, you must remain in that node's space. Moving to a different node immediately ends your active control, though any shutdown effects you've created will persist.

The choice between shutdown and active control often depends on your team's broader tactical situation. Shutting down a security system might be sufficient if you just need to prevent it from hindering your allies. However, actively controlling that same system could provide crucial advantages - but only if you can maintain your position despite the threats moving through the digital space.

### Boon Nodes

As you thread your way through the digital landscape, you may discover opportunities to patch into specialized data caches - digital artifacts known among threadweavers as "Boon Nodes." Experienced hackers keep an eye out for these valuable resources, recognizing their signature code patterns that can be repurposed to gain tactical advantages:

- **Restoration Node** (Blue glow): Heals XdX damage when passed through.
- **Amplification Node** (Green glow): Grants +X to your next skill check or saving throw while in the system.
- **Velocity Node** (Yellow glow): Increases your movement by X nodes on your next turn.
- **Disruption Node** (Purple glow): The next Fireward you encounter has disadvantage on its next check or save against you.
- **Conversion Node** (White glow): The next Fireward you successfully defeat doesn't get destroyed - instead, it becomes a temporary ally that follows your mental commands.

Strategic threadweavers often plan their routes to incorporate these powerful resources, sometimes prioritizing a slightly longer path to gain crucial advantages for challenging sections ahead.

# Threats and Security

If hacking into a system were easy, everyone would do it. However, most Weave Networks have security built within.

## Firewards

The most common threat you'll encounter while threadweaving are Firewards - autonomous magical programs that patrol and protect Weave Systems. These digital sentinels are represented by hexagonal tokens that display two crucial pieces of information: a modifier at the top that represents the Fireward's skill bonus, and a damage value at the bottom.

![[Images/fireward-token.png| center |400]]

Firewards, like you, have the ability to move (but only one space per turn). When you enter the same node as a Fireward (or when a Fireward moves into your node), all movement immediately stops. You and the Fireward make opposing skill checks. You will roll a _hacking tools_ check, while the Fireward rolls with its designated modifier.

Once you have successfully claimed your target node(s), most firewards cannot reclaim these nodes from you. This ensures that progress through the system is maintained even when facing persistent security measures.

### Specialized Firewards

Beyond standard Firewards, Weave Systems employ specialized security measures with unique behaviors and interactions:

- **Barrier Wards (Red)**: These immobile red Firewards project energy fields that block pathways until defeated. Unlike other Firewards, Barrier Wards remain stationary. When you encounter a Barrier Ward, make opposing thives tool checks againced the ward). Success reduces its hit counter by 1 (most have 2-3 hit counters), and destroying it clears the path. Failure forces you back to your previous node.

- **Trace Wards (Blue)**: These shimmering blue sensor arrays constantly sweep for unauthorized access. When you enter a node with a Trace Ward, make a Dexterity (Stealth) check against the ward's passive perception. Success lets you slip past undetected, temporarily disabling it. Failure triggers an alert, spawning 1d4 standard Firewards at entry points over the next 1d4 rounds.

- **Drain Wards (Violet)**: These violet energy wards target your magical essence. When encountering a Drain Ward, make an Intelligence saving throw against the DC displayed on the ward. Failure causes you to lose one spell slot of a level equal the ward's level (minimum 1). Success destroys the ward immediately.

- **Echo Wards (Silver)**: These resonating silver wards always appear in pairs, connected by threads of matching energy. They're distinguished by matching symbols on their tokens. When you encounter an Echo Ward, make a Hacking Tools check against its displayed DC. Failure teleports you to its paired ward elsewhere in the system. Success destroys both connected Echo Wards simultaneously.

## Rival Threadweavers

While Firewards are dangerous, they're ultimately predictable. The same cannot be said for rival threadweavers. Unlike Firewards, rival threadweavers operate under the same rules you do - they're conscious hackers who can move, claim nodes, and engage in digital combat. They move up to their Intelligence modifier in nodes per turn, can claim and control treasure nodes, and are equally vulnerable to psychic damage. When a rival threadweaver takes control over a node, you must use your action to once again take control.

When you enter the same node as a rival threadweaver (or they enter yours), the encounter plays out similarly to a Fireward - movement stops and you make opposing Hacking Tools checks. However, there are two key differences: instead of being destroyed on a failed check, the losing threadweaver takes psychic damage as you would and is pushed back to their previous node. Both threadweavers' turns end after the encounter, regardless of the outcome.

> [!warning] System Stakes
> Perhaps the most dangerous aspect of rival threadweavers is their ability to seize control of treasure nodes. A security system under enemy control is far more dangerous than one that's simply active. While an active defensive turret system might automatically shoot individuals who pass by within range, turrets under control of a rival Threadweaver can move and control the turret, turning it against your party. Sometimes defending a crucial node takes priority over pushing forward to claim new ones.

## Strategic Withdrawal

Once you've accomplished your primary objectives, you should consider your exit strategy. If a threadweavers only goal was to shut down machines, players can immediately jack out the moment they reach the final treasure node. While remaining jacked in allows for continued control of certain systems, it also leaves your physical form vulnerable.

> [!tip] Emergency Disconnect
> If the situation becomes dire while you're deep in a system, you can attempt an emergency disconnect from any node by using your action and making a Hacking Tools check. The DC is based on system complexity and your distance from an entry point. Success allows you to safely jack out, while failure might result in psychic damage or other system countermeasures.
>
> This option ensures you're never truly trapped within a system, but the safest method remains returning to an entry point for a controlled disconnect.

Remember that rival threadweavers can still claim nodes you've already secured, potentially undoing your work. Consider whether maintaining a presence in the system to defend key nodes is worth the risk to your physical form.
